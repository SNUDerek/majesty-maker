**Diogenes:** "The Divine is not found in perfectly straight lines, girls! It is in the roots of the earth and the erratic dance of the stars!"

### Beat 1b: The Deacon (Activity: Scriptorium Job)

**Narration:** The abbey scriptorium is freezing, silent, and flawlessly organized. Deacon Jerome stands at the front, inspecting the inkwells.
**Jerome:** "Every letter you transcribe is a brick in the fortress of faith. A single error is a crack where the darkness enters. Accuracy is holiness."
**[CONDITIONAL: if Melancholy > 30]**

* **Then:** **Narration:** The sheer weight of the silence and the rigid expectations presses down on you, making it hard to breathe.
* **Else:** **Narration:** You find the strict quiet of the stone room surprisingly calming after the chaos of the outside world.

**Daughter:** *(He means well, but he speaks of faith as if it were a prison wall. There is no joy in his voice at all.)*
**Daughter:** [CHOICE]

* **Option A (Dutiful):** "I will be careful, Deacon Jerome. My hand will not slip."
* **Option B (Questioning):** "But surely the Saints understand if a tired scribe makes a small mistake?"

**Jerome:** "The Saints demand our absolute best, child. We do not lower the standard to accommodate human weakness. Take your seat."

### Beat 2: The Scriptorium Rules (Activity: Scriptorium Job)

**Narration:** You finish grinding a fresh batch of expensive lapis lazuli ink, intending to use it for an illuminated manuscript. Serena steps in front of you.

**Jerome:** "Serena is correct. The rules preserve the hierarchy of learning. Give her the ink, and return to the psalms."
**Daughter:** *(She isn't breaking the rules; she's weaponizing them to keep herself at the top and me at the bottom.)*
**Daughter:** [CHOICE]

* **Option A (Resentful):** "Fine. Take it. I hope your illuminations are as rigid as your personality."

### Beat 3: The Heathen Text (Activity: Oratores Tutor)

**Narration:** Diogenes drops a massive, dust-covered tome onto the table. It is written in ancient Greek.
**Diogenes:** "The *Materia Medica* of Dioscorides! Pre-dates the Church by centuries. Pay attention to the chapter on the white willow tree!"
**Serena:** "A pagan text? Absolutely not. I will not read heathen superstitions when Galenic medicine is sanctioned by the abbey."
**[CONDITIONAL: if Oratores > 40]**

* **Then:** **Narration:** You smoothly translate the Greek, realizing the text isn't superstition at all, but highly detailed botanical science.
* **Else:** **Narration:** You struggle with the unfamiliar alphabet, but Diogenes points out detailed, practical sketches of herbs and roots.

**Daughter:** *(She is blinding herself on purpose. She would rather be 'correct' than actually learn something new.)*
**Daughter:** [CHOICE]

* **Option A (Direct):** "Serena, this describes how to break a fever using tree bark. It isn't magic, it's practical medicine."
* **Option B (Let it go):** "Suit yourself. But I'm going to read it. Knowledge is knowledge."

**Serena:** "The abbey provides all the knowledge necessary for salvation and health. I will pray for your misguided soul."

### Beat 4: The Climax (Activity: Scriptorium Job)

**Narration:** A desperate commoner is brought into the abbey's hospice wing, shivering violently with the ague. Jerome and Serena are at his bedside.
**Jerome:** "His humors are terribly imbalanced. Apply the heated cups, Serena, and pray for the Saints to intervene."
**Serena:** "I have applied them, Deacon, but the fever only rises! The orthodox texts say he should be recovering by now!"
**[CONDITIONAL: if Oratores > 50]**

* **Then:** **Narration:** You don't hesitate. You rush to the apothecary cabinet, immediately recognizing the dried white willow bark from Dioscorides' text.
* **Else:** **Narration:** You scramble to remember Diogenes' lesson, desperately pulling out a bundle of pale bark you hope is the right one.

**Daughter:** "The heated cups are only dehydrating him! He needs a tea steeped from this willow bark. It will break the fever."
**Serena:** "That is unsanctioned heathen medicine! Deacon Jerome, forbid her!"
**Daughter:** *(If we wait for a miracle, he dies. We have to act!)*
**Daughter:** [CHOICE]

* **Option A (Take Charge):** "I don't care if it's unsanctioned! Help me brew this, Serena, or his blood is on your hands!"

**Narration:** Serena hesitates, looking at the dying man, then at you. Her rigid posture breaks. She grabs a kettle and helps you brew the tea.

### Beat 5: The Resolution (Activity: Oratores Tutor)

**Narration:** Diogenes’ study. The commoner's fever broke the night before. Serena stands quietly by the window, looking humbled.
**Serena:** "Deacon Jerome was furious... but the man lived. I realize now that my rules were a shield against the messy reality of the world."
